## Purpose of This Briefing

This document addresses judicial concerns regarding the deployment of artificial intelligence (AI) technology in legal education through the LawHub Uganda platform. It provides assurances on:

1. The non-interference of AI with judicial independence and discretion
2. Chain of custody and statutory authority for legal documents
3. The distinction between assistive and determinative AI
4. Citation verification and legal accuracy safeguards
5. Compliance with constitutional principles governing the judiciary

---

## 1. Judicial Independence — Article 126 Assurance

### Constitutional Foundation

Article 126(1) of the Constitution of Uganda, 1995, provides:

> *"Judicial power is derived from the people and shall be exercised by the courts established under this Constitution in the name of the people and in conformity with law and with the values, norms and aspirations of the people."*

Article 128(1) further guarantees:

> *"In the exercise of judicial power, the courts shall be independent and shall not be subject to the control or direction of any person or authority."*

### LawHub's Position

LawHub Uganda **categorically does not**:

- Make, recommend, or influence judicial decisions
- Provide legal advice to parties in litigation
- Generate outputs intended for court submission without advocate review
- Predict case outcomes or sentencing recommendations
- Automate any function that falls within the domain of judicial discretion

LawHub operates exclusively as an **educational research tool** for law students and academic staff. It has no connection to court case management systems, judicial decision-support systems, or any component of the formal justice delivery chain.

## 2. Chain of Custody for Legal Documents

### Document Integrity Framework

All legal documents within LawHub are subject to a multi-layered integrity process:

| Layer | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| **Source verification** | Constitution text, statutes, and case law sourced from the Uganda Legal Information Institute (ULII) and the Uganda Gazette |
| **Editorial review** | Verified by the LawHub Editorial Board before publication |
| **Audit trail** | Every document records: creator, creation date, last update, verification status, and reviewer identity |
| **Immutability** | Published documents cannot be modified by students or lecturers — only Administrators with explicit review |
| **Version tracking** | Constitutional amendments are annotated with Act name, year, and affected articles |

### Academic Material Workflow

Lecturer-submitted academic materials follow a formal review pipeline:

```
Lecturer submits material (PENDING_REVIEW)
        │
        ▼
Administrator reviews
        │
        ├── APPROVED → Published to student library
        ├── CHANGES_REQUESTED → Returned to lecturer
        └── REJECTED → Removed from queue
```

No academic material is published without explicit Administrator approval.

## 3. Statutory Authority and Citation Verification

### Statutory References

LawHub's legal database references the following authoritative sources:

| Source | Authority |
|---|---|
| The Constitution of the Republic of Uganda, 1995 | Supreme law under Article 2 |
| Acts of Parliament | Enacted under Article 79 |
| Statutory Instruments | Made under delegated legislative authority |
| Supreme Court judgements | Binding precedent under the doctrine of stare decisis |
| Court of Appeal judgements | Binding on the High Court and subordinate courts |
| High Court judgements | Persuasive authority |

### AI Citation Safeguards

The AI system is bound by explicit instructions to:

1. **Cite only authentic Ugandan authorities** — Articles of the Constitution, specific sections of Acts, and named judicial decisions with citations
2. **Never fabricate citations** — If a citation is unavailable, the AI states the principle and directs the user to verify on ULII
3. **Distinguish clearly** between statutory text, judicial dicta, and academic commentary
4. **Include mandatory disclaimer** — Every AI response states it is for educational purposes only and does not constitute legal advice

## 4. Assistive vs. Determinative AI

### Critical Distinction

LawHub deploys AI in a strictly **assistive** capacity:

| Characteristic | Assistive AI (LawHub) ✅ | Determinative AI ❌ |
|---|---|---|
| **Function** | Helps users understand and locate legal principles | Makes binding decisions |
| **Output authority** | Advisory and educational only | Binding and enforceable |
| **Human oversight** | Required for all AI outputs | Replaces human judgement |
| **Court relevance** | None — not designed for court proceedings | Would interfere with judicial process |
| **Disclaimer** | Present on every AI response | Not applicable |

### Practical Boundaries

| AI Feature | What It Does | What It Does NOT Do |
|---|---|---|
| **AI Tutor** | Explains Article 21 (equality before the law) using the structured IRAC method | Does not advise a litigant on their Article 21 claim |
| **Legal Drafter** | Generates a template affidavit for educational drafting practice | Does not produce court-ready documents — requires advocate endorsement |
| **Quiz Generator** | Creates practice MCQs on Constitutional Law topics | Does not assess or certify legal competence |

## 6. Safeguards Summary

### Technical Safeguards

- AI system prompts are version-controlled and subject to academic review
- Temperature parameters set conservatively (0.3–0.5) to minimise speculative outputs
- Three-model fallback cascade ensures reliability without altering response quality
- All AI responses filtered through Google's built-in safety systems

### Institutional Safeguards

- Chief Legal Administrator reviews and approves all system prompts
- Academic Council sets content standards for legal accuracy
- AI governance framework documented and available for inspection
- Regular bias assessments conducted (semi-annually)

### User Safeguards

- Mandatory educational disclaimer on every AI response
- Clear labelling of AI-generated vs. human-authored content
- No AI-generated content automatically published or submitted
- Users directed to ULII for authoritative verification

## 7. Addressing Judicial Concerns

### Concern: "Will AI replace legal reasoning?"

**Response:** No. LawHub's AI is a study aid equivalent to a digital textbook or research assistant. It helps students structure their legal analysis using the IRAC method but does not substitute for independent legal reasoning, moot court advocacy, or clinical legal practice.

### Concern: "Can AI outputs be presented in court?"

**Response:** LawHub explicitly prohibits this. Every AI-drafted document includes a prominent disclaimer: *"This legal document must be reviewed, verified, and endorsed by an Advocate of the High Court of Uganda prior to execution or court filing."* The platform has no integration with court filing systems.

### Concern: "Is the legal data accurate?"

**Response:** All legal data is sourced from ULII, the Uganda Gazette, and official court reports. The platform bundles the complete 1995 Constitution (288 articles, 19 chapters, 7 schedules) and verified statutes. AI-generated explanations are supplementary to, not replacements for, the primary statutory and judicial texts.

### Concern: "Who controls the AI?"

**Response:** The AI operates under deterministic system prompts authored by the LawHub Academic Council, reviewed quarterly. The prompts are committed to version control, auditable, and cannot be modified by end users. Temperature parameters, model selection, and content boundaries are set by the engineering team under academic oversight.

## Conclusion

LawHub Uganda is a legal **education** platform — not a legal **practice** or **judicial decision-support** tool. It operates within clearly defined boundaries that respect:

- The supremacy of the Constitution under Article 2
- The independence of the judiciary under Articles 126 and 128
- The interpretive authority of the Constitutional Court under Article 137
- The right to a fair hearing under Article 28
- Data protection rights under the DPPA 2019

The platform invites judicial inspection and welcomes feedback from judicial officers on how it can further strengthen its safeguards and educational value.
